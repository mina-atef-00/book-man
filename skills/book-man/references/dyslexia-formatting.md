# Dyslexia Formatting Guidelines

Grounded in British Dyslexia Association (BDA) style guide, WCAG 2.2, NHS Clear Language standards.

## Typography
- **Font:** Use sans-serif (Arial, Verdana, Tahoma) — serif fonts blur for dyslexic readers
- **Size:** 12-14pt minimum
- **Line spacing:** 1.5x minimum
- **Paragraph spacing:** 1.5x line height between paragraphs
- **Max line length:** 60-70 characters
- **Alignment:** Left-aligned, never justified (uneven spacing creates "rivers of white")

## Writing Style
- **Short sentences:** Under 25 words where possible
- **Short paragraphs:** 3-4 sentences max
- **Active voice:** "The author argues" not "It is argued by the author"
- **Concrete over abstract:** "Smith observed 200 patients" not "Empirical observation was conducted"
- **One idea per paragraph:** Split complex topics into digestible chunks

## Visual Structure
- **Bold** for key terms on first mention (not italics — italics slant makes letters harder to recognize)
- **Bullet points** for lists of 2+ items
- **Numbered lists** for sequential steps
- **Clear heading hierarchy:** H1 → H2 → H3 (never skip levels)
- **White space** between sections
- **Avoid:** ALL CAPS (word shapes are lost), italics (letter slant), underlining (crowding)

## Colors
- **Background:** Off-white/cream (#FAF9F8) preferred over pure white
- **Text:** Dark gray (#333) preferred over pure black (reduces contrast glare)
- **Avoid:** Green/red color pairs (most common color blindness)
- **Links:** Underline + distinct color, not color alone

## Content Structure
1. **Start with the conclusion** — what should the reader know?
2. **Support with evidence** — citations, data, examples
3. **End with summary** — reinforce key takeaways
4. **Use examples** for abstract concepts
5. **Mindmaps** for relationships between concepts
